## Verdict: NOT READY for a go-live that sends for real (two HIGH, both in the new D-222 S2 code; one narrow fix round)

The place work (M7, M8, L5, home country, S1, `consent_both_known`) and everything the three earlier passes confirmed hold. The HIGH findings come from one new function, `status_question` (always.py:3694; `decide` always.py:5010, overrides at about 5018 and 5079), which is a keyword detector: any yes/no field or tick box whose label holds a country name or demonym plus a status word anywhere is taken as "a status question about the owner", **whatever else the label says**. `decide` (diff +618..+623) then replaces the code key and the model's class with `work_right:status`. Consequences, all reproduced:

* **H1: a consent question or consent box that mentions a status word is ticked / answered Yes from the owner's nationality, with every consent false.**
* **H2: questions that are not "are you a citizen" are answered from the nationality fact** (another person's status, National Service, scholarship, passport validity, "renounced", "former", "intend to apply"), the class `other_person` and the National Service key are overridden, the confirmer is fed an irrelevant WANTED sentence, and the answer cites `profile:singapore_status.nationality` as its source.

D-212 said word lists cannot close this kind of reading and a vote can; S2 adds a word list whose output (the owner's truth sentence) steers the vote itself.

### MEDIUM

**M1. A second place that code does not recognise, after a function word, is not caught by either key (L5 residual).**
`complete_place_follow` stops at the first function word; code's own reading flags an unknown place only after "in" / "within". So a quote "Singapore" is accepted when a city or other unrecognised place follows `as well as`, `in addition to`, `then`, `also`, `after`, `from`, `to`, `at`, `for the`, `during`, `as opposed to`, `but not`. Reproduced (`p8.py`, `p9.py`; the model quotes only "Singapore", answers Yes, the confirmer agrees): **Yes (Singapore's truth) written** for "Are you authorised to work in Singapore as well as Mumbai?", "... in addition to Mumbai", "... then Mumbai", "... also Mumbai", "... after Mumbai", "... from Mumbai / to Mumbai", "... but not Mumbai", "... as opposed to Mumbai", "... for the Mumbai team", "... at our Mumbai hub", "... as a Berlin resident", "... until you move to our Tokyo office", "... for our Auckland office", "... after a transfer to Zurich", "... while ... " variants without "in". 24 of 87 probes. With a named country the second place IS caught ("Singapore as well as the US", "... from Malaysia", "... before transferring to the US": empty). With "in <city>" caught (unknown place). Every join word `and / or / plus / along with / together with / including / & / , / -` is rejected.
Severity: by the bar's letter a wrong protected write; by likelihood it needs an unusual wording AND a model that quotes only the first place (the real model quotes whole phrases for open places but quotes "Singapore" for plain ones). I rate it MEDIUM and would not block on it, but the fix is small: in `quoted_place`, also reject a quote when any later lower-case-or-capitalised word run after the first function word is not frame vocabulary AND code reads any capitalised token that is not the job country / a frame word (a capitalised word after the quote that is not a function word means a name). Alternative: treat a capitalised unknown word anywhere in a work-rights label as an unknown place (the Q-121 rehearsal pages use capitalised names only for places and employers).

**M2. Plain Singapore wordings with a time / condition tail are emptied by the L5 follower rule (availability).**
30 wordings (`p20.py`: the real model quotes "Singapore"; fake right model; faithful votes): 18 answered / 12 empty (idealised), 17 / 13 (realistic). Empty: "... work in Singapore effective immediately", "... beginning January", "... throughout the contract period", "... throughout the internship (May to August)", "... ahead of the start date", "... regardless of your nationality", "... , including overtime", "... , and can you provide documents on request?", "Do you have the right to work in Singapore? Please answer honestly." (a hint sentence appended: "please" is not a function word), "Do you require sponsorship to work in Singapore once your studies end?" (a common internship wording). These are not the most common forms (the 45 typical wordings all pass: section "Availability"), but "Please ...", "once ...", "throughout", "effective" appear on real Greenhouse / Workday pages; the page then ends empty and a required field fails the application (fail-closed, no wrong answer).
Fix: add a closed list of time / condition words to `_PURE_CONDITIONS` (throughout, once, ahead, effective, immediately, beginning, starting, regardless, please, honestly, now, later, currently, including is NOT one: it joins). Pin the 10 rows.
